\begin{abstract}
Dynamic content breaks RGB-D SLAM in a specific way: a moving object supplies residuals
that a rigid-world estimator resolves by moving the camera. Run unmodified, our
3DGS-SLAM backbone drifts to $39.32\pm1.01$ cm on a mixed-mover sequence and
$147.5\pm39.0$ cm on a crowded one, against roughly 1.5 cm on static scenes. The
dominant response detects movers with an external class-conditioned network and discards
them, inheriting a second model's cost and failure modes, and is closed-set. We instead
decide which pixels to trust from the disagreement between observed optical flow and the
flow a rigid scene would produce, down-weighting rather than removing, and report the
measurements that bound the result. A pre-registered three-factor factorial (120 runs) finds that component
necessity is \emph{regime-dependent}: no sequence needs all three components, and uniform
keyframe coverage is harmful on the mixed-mover scene. A controlled
counterpart consuming byte-identical frozen flow through a naive threshold is unstable
where ours is not, closing the reading that the gain follows from the extra information.
Three pose-side endpoints, one a directly measured intermediate quantity, show a
variance-bias trade in which per-frame tracking worsens while the trajectory improves. We
give the boundary where our mask-free configuration stops being sufficient, and claim no
state-of-the-art accuracy.

\keywords{Dynamic SLAM \and 3D Gaussian splatting \and Robust estimation.}
\end{abstract}
